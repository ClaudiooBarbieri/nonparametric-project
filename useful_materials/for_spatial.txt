Dealing with spatial dependencies in non-parametric models, especially for bulk density prediction along the LUCAS points, requires addressing the inherent correlation between geographically close points. Here are some approaches to consider:

### 1. **Explicitly Model Spatial Dependency**
   - Use **geostatistical methods** like **Kriging**:
     - Kriging explicitly models spatial autocorrelation through a variogram. It can predict values at unmeasured locations while accounting for spatial dependencies.
   - Incorporate **spatial kernels** in your non-parametric model:
     - For example, extend Kernel Regression or Gaussian Processes (GP) with kernels designed for spatial data, such as the **Matérn kernel** or other distance-based kernels.

### 2. **Use Spatial Cross-Validation**
   - Traditional cross-validation assumes IID samples, which is violated here. Instead:
     - Use **spatial blocking**: Divide data into spatial blocks, ensuring points from the same area are either all in the training or test set. This prevents "data leakage" from nearby points.
     - Apply **leave-one-block-out** cross-validation: Select blocks as test sets iteratively.

### 3. **Incorporate Covariates to Capture Spatial Effects**
   - Include spatial covariates (e.g., latitude, longitude, elevation, climate data, soil characteristics) in the model to account for spatial variation indirectly.
   - Use **spatial embeddings** to encode geographic relationships in a machine learning framework.

### 4. **Use Spatial Regularization**
   - Regularize your model with spatial penalties to enforce smoothness or consistency over neighboring areas. For instance:
     - Apply penalties in kernel density estimates to limit extreme variations in spatially close predictions.

### 5. **Correct for Spatial Autocorrelation in Residuals**
   - After fitting your model:
     - Check residuals for spatial autocorrelation using **Moran's I** or similar statistics.
     - If significant autocorrelation exists, iterate by refining spatial features or switching to a more spatially-aware modeling approach.

### 6. **Non-Stationarity and Local Models**
   - Use **local regression techniques** like LOESS or geographically weighted regression (GWR):
     - These methods allow model parameters to vary spatially, capturing local patterns better.
   - Consider partitioning the area into smaller subregions and fitting separate models.

### 7. **Combine with Conformal Prediction**
   - To address uncertainty and improve robustness:
     - Apply **conformal prediction** in a spatially-aware way, ensuring prediction intervals adapt based on spatial variation.

### 8. **Test for Independence Before Assuming IID**
   - Use statistical tests (e.g., **Mantel test**) to quantify the spatial correlation and understand its impact on your predictions.
